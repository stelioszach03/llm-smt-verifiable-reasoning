\begin{abstract}
This document describes an implemented candidate-verification pipeline and a
planned study of solver feedback in language-model repair. It is an evaluation
protocol, not a paper reporting completed experiments. No advantage over a
baseline is established.
\end{abstract}
\section{Implemented interface}
A generator proposes a satisfying assignment or an UNSAT claim for supplied
formal constraints. Z3 checks the candidate against that encoding. A repair
loop may receive structured verification feedback. Solver success certifies
only the supplied constraints, not the correctness of a natural-language
interpretation. A timeout is unknown, not proof of infeasibility.
\section{Planned comparison}
The pipeline implements \texttt{one\_shot}, \texttt{multi\_no\_feedback},
\texttt{multi\_generic\_feedback}, \texttt{multi\_unsat\_core\_feedback}, and
\texttt{cd\_vgs\_core\_rank}. The full script specifies 500 synthetic linear
problems, three seeds and five arms. Each method must be evaluated under a
recorded actual model/engine and fixed protocol. Shared round and candidate
caps are resource limits; measured usage is needed to assess cost comparability.

\section{Reproducibility requirements}
Preserve input hashes, model and server revisions, decoding settings, prompts,
candidate outputs, solver outcomes, repair decisions, errors, token counts and
latency. Aggregate results must be traceable to those records. Small Sudoku,
Latin-square and scheduling examples exercise adapters; they are not evidence
of broad reasoning generalization.
